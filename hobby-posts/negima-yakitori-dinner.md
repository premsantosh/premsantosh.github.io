This one started with boredom. It was a slow evening, I was craving yakitori, and the one spot nearby that does it properly had no open reservations. Rather than sulk about it, I decided the best table in town was my own — so I threaded the skewers, put on the miso soup, and cooked the meal I couldn't book.

Negima is one of the great classics of the yakitori canon: bite-size pieces of chicken thigh threaded onto bamboo skewers with segments of scallion in between, grilled and lacquered with tare — the sweet-savory glaze of soy sauce, mirin, and sake that caramelizes into that dark, glossy char. The name is a little compression of *negi* (the scallion) and *ma* (the space between) — the dish is literally named after the gap the onion fills. Yakitori took off as street food in Meiji-era Japan, when eating chicken finally went mainstream, and the oldest yakitori joints famously never empty their tare pot — they just keep topping it up, some for decades, so every skewer carries a bit of history. Mine has no such pedigree yet, but rounded out with miso soup and quick cucumber pickles in rice-wine vinegar (plus a few edible flowers, because why not), it did the job the restaurant couldn't.
